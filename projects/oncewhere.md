# oncewhere

## Question

What should remain available after a shared experience ends, and what form lets someone return to it?

## What exists

oncewhere is a creative project about turning real experiences and relationships into things that can be understood, experienced, and kept. Its first work, 月背拾遗司 · 第一夜, is a complete playable first build with five chapters, object puzzles, an original piano sequence, sound, and a standalone collectible HTML version. The work is still being polished.

The project itself states an important boundary: the current artifact is a delivery format, not a permanent commitment to one technology, and real audience or music validation has not yet been claimed.

## My role

This is my own creative line. Its relevance to this atlas is personal and thematic: I care about persistence, return, and the form in which an experience remains available.

## Evidence

See the [repository](https://github.com/yuan-chengzhi/oncewhere), the [first-work overview](https://github.com/yuan-chengzhi/oncewhere/tree/main/works/moon-office), and the [published first-build commit](https://github.com/yuan-chengzhi/oncewhere/commit/9ea4c02316cdd96ca5c4f8f6d7a0af2bdcfe240).

## What this establishes

A real experience can be turned into a playable and preservable artifact while leaving room for later forms.

## What this does not establish

oncewhere is not a technical component of, or validation for, the mathematical-agent system.

## What changed in my research direction

It keeps the central repository from becoming only a technical catalogue. The same intuition about what should survive an immediate moment appears here in a human and creative register.

## Next question

What forms let a shared experience remain returnable without freezing it into one medium?
