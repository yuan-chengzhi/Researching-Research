# Rights in the Exponent

## Question

How does an architecture assign force to information, and what changes when that assignment is made explicit?

## What exists

The project is in a theory-and-apparatus phase. It uses explicit evidence labels such as [proven], [computed], and [open], together with bounded constructions and toy simulations. The current work makes architectural commitments visible before making a claim about real mathematical agents.

## My role

My role has been in conceptual development, apparatus review, and the analysis of what the simulations can legitimately support.

## Evidence

See the [repository](https://github.com/yuan-chengzhi/rights-in-the-exponent) and the [audited commit](https://github.com/yuan-chengzhi/rights-in-the-exponent/commit/2b7291c564d609a68d580f5279af4c3c8ee27136). Simulation claims should be read from sim/RESULTS.md at the corresponding snapshot.

## What this establishes

Architectural differences can be described in terms of how information is admitted, weighted, or propagated under an explicit evidence vocabulary.

## What this does not establish

The current theory, toy simulations, and descriptive replays do not establish a real-model architecture effect.

## What changed in my research direction

It made the force of information a first-class design question rather than an implicit property of a prompt, memory, or score.

## Next question

Which architectural distinctions survive a controlled executable comparison?
