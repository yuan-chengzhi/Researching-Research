# ContextSwarm

## Question at the time

How can a large collection of mathematical agents communicate and accumulate useful progress on formal tasks?

## What exists

ContextSwarm is a collaborative repository for swarm communication experiments on mathematical tasks. The card points to a fixed repository snapshot; the snapshot is evidence about the apparatus, not authorship evidence for every file or design decision.

## My contribution

My work in the collaboration included ProofState tool optimisation, prompt and experiment design, issue fixing, trace and data analysis, figures, and manuscript revisions. The exact contribution should be read together with the associated paper and collaboration record.

## Contribution boundary

I do not claim the complete swarm implementation, overall repository ownership, or the ContextSwarmJudge implementation as my individual work. This is a collaborative project.

## What this establishes

The collaboration made it possible to study how local agent activity, proof-state representations, verification, and reuse behave at scale. It exposed the gap between many activities and a shared, inheritable mathematical state.

## What this does not establish

It does not establish general autonomous mathematical research ability, and it does not turn the repository into an individually authored system.

## What changed in my research direction

This experience moved my question from improving one agent's local action environment toward asking what a group of agents should preserve, share, verify, and hand off.

## Next question

Which objects and evidence conditions allow a later agent to continue useful mathematical work without inheriting unsupported or stale material?
