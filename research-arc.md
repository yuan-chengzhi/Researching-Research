## A problem map, not a predetermined workflow

My recurring interest is how environments and organization let agents use their capabilities and turn local work into collective progress. Formal mathematics is the principal current setting. The questions below interact; they are not a maturity ladder or a claim that every later idea was planned at the beginning.

| Question | Concrete entry points | Distinction to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Collective behavior and progress | Collective Acceleration, ContextSwarm | Shared activity is not automatically useful collective work. |
| Reliable action and feedback | Putnam, Pi Math First Proof Swarm, pi-lean-tools, TacticWorld | Useful tools and prescribed work order are different design choices. |
| Unfinished and persistent work | Metron, Persistent Mathematical Worlds, MathWeave | Stored or retrieved material is not automatically accepted mathematics. |
| Organization and replacement | PMW Research Platform, Aperture, Rights in the Exponent | Replacement has specific state, interface, and evidence contracts. |
| Evidence and diagnosis | FLT/AAMAS analysis, Agent Math Frontier, Trace analysis | An observed recurrence is not a causal explanation. |
| Autonomous system evolution | Current Q0 and Q8 | Permission to change a system does not establish effective improvement. |

## One campaign, distinct outputs

ContextSwarm is the central collaborative FLT project. Its system and FLTBench produced an ICLR-stage submission; subsequent large-run analysis formed the AAMAS branch. The branches have different contributions and evidence but are not two independently built systems. Full FLT formalization remains unfinished. ContextSwarmJudge is an associated reference, not a repository contribution of mine.

## Current direction

Can the system discover opportunities, propose, implement, test, and retain useful changes, and improve how it searches for and evaluates them? Shared-state design, tools, communication, division of work, and evaluation procedures may all be investigated. Each experiment needs a bounded scope; the entire agenda need not be reduced to a single substrate mutation.

Resource allocation between task work and improvement, the cost of maintaining changes, and the diversity of competing directions are open organizational questions. Benefits should be tested against appropriate alternatives on subsequent independent tasks under stated total budgets and reliable evaluation. See [the future direction](docs/research-plan.md) and [current questions](current-questions.md).

## What counts as progress in this atlas

A useful addition connects an actual artifact to a question, evidence, a personal contribution, and a bounded interpretation that could be revised. More repositories alone are not progress. oncewhere stays beside this technical arc as a creative and personal exploration, not as validation of the mathematical-agent system.
