# 0003 — Broad research scope, explicit contributions, and a shorter reader path

Date: 2026-10-14  
Status: proposed for PR review; research hypotheses remain open.

## Context

The earlier atlas preserved valuable projects and evidence boundaries, but its entry pages increasingly equated the future agenda with changing a proof-work substrate or a single shared-state choice. The author's current formulation is broader: collective behavior, organization, capability scaling, and autonomous discovery, implementation, testing, and retention of system changes, including improvement of the change process itself.

The homepage also mixed all indexed entries with selected work. ContextSwarmJudge's collaborative label implied a repository contribution the author does not claim. Putnam's source commit was still unpinned, and its runtime wording could be mistaken for total end-to-end elapsed time.

## Decision

Use a concise bilingual homepage with selected actual work, concrete changes in understanding, and a collapsed full index. Keep every existing project ID and historical decision. Treat ICLR and AAMAS as outputs of one FLT campaign; treat tools and the experiment they support as related artifacts rather than extra achievements.

Reclassify ContextSwarmJudge as reference-only throughout the card, registry, diagrams, and rendering. Pin the inspected Putnam report and A5 comparison. Explain the internal verification criterion, serial-problem/parallel-attempt setup, and summed-runtime denominator. Do not copy sensitive raw source details.

Add Collective Acceleration with author-report evidence and TacticWorld with a pinned design reference and unfinished-training status. These complement the map without claiming maturity or an artificial chronological dependency.

Broaden Q0 while retaining Q1–Q6; add Q7 for local-to-collective progress and Q8 for resource allocation and evolutionary diversity. The earlier state/substrate questions remain useful experimental slices, not the exclusive definition of the research object. A fixed four-arm or one-mutation first experiment is no longer represented as a settled commitment.
